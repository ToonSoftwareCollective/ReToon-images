      How this works

      Use OpenTherm looks for OpenTherm (about 5 seconds); without it the on/off contact stays in use. Once OpenTherm has worked on this Toon it stays OpenTherm: when the installation stops answering, the Toon keeps the OpenTherm line going and keeps trying, and shows that it does not answer. Only you switch to the on/off contact, here.

      An ESP32 or ESP8266 with an OpenTherm adapter at the boiler or heat pump, running ESPHome, instead of the boiler module and its cable. The Toon keeps doing the thermostat work and reaches the ESP over WiFi, encrypted. Fill in the board and the adapter's pins, choose Use this ESP, and install the configuration that appears below with ESPHome.

      Fill in the pins as your adapter's description gives them for ESPHome (in_pin and out_pin).

      Some adapters have a small temperature sensor (DS18B20). The module usually sits next to the installation, so it is no room temperature: it comes in Home Assistant as "OpenTherm module temperature", and can be chosen there like any other sensor.

      The configuration for ESPHome: make a new device in ESPHome (the Home Assistant add-on or ESPHome on a computer), replace its configuration by this one and install it, the first time over USB. The WiFi name and password come from ESPHome's secrets (wifi_ssid and wifi_password). It holds this Toon's key for the ESP: keep it to yourself.

      Nothing connected to the Toon: "Heat request" and "Cool request" in Home Assistant (and on the screen) follow the thermostat, and an automation there switches a heater, a heat pump or an airco. The thermostat still decides when to heat or cool. Switch cooling on under Cooling.

      Smart heating

      Heating starts when the flow temperature asked for is above this. Standard 30; lower it (to 25) for floor heating or a heat pump. An installation that cannot run that low may switch on and off often.

      With the box above off: the installation heats with this flow temperature, and the thermostat only switches the heating on and off. Empty: it follows how far the room is from the target.

      Switch this on when your heating is connected over OpenTherm but the flow temperature cannot be set, as with most district heating sets: hot water arrives whatever the thermostat asks for. You can tell by the room temperature: every time the heating stops, also close to the target, the room keeps getting warmer for a long while (it overshoots). With this on, the thermostat switches the heat on in short pulses, as for an on/off boiler, and learns how the house warms up that way. The other OpenTherm values (hot water, faults) stay. Leave it off for an ordinary gas boiler or a heat pump: those do follow the temperature.

      Lower this for a slow heating system such as floor heating.

      On/off heating is either fully on or off. So the thermostat switches it on for part of every cycle, as much as the house needs: for example 4 of the 20 minutes when the room is nearly warm enough, the whole cycle when it is still cold. When the house cools faster than expected, it comes on again within the same cycle. Shorter: a steadier temperature, but it starts more often. Longer: fewer starts, for a slow system such as floor heating. Standard 20.

      Maximum flow temperature

      Outside (°C)Flow at most (°C)

      Between these two points the maximum follows a straight line; colder or warmer, it stays at the end value. Without an outside temperature the cold value is used.

      For OpenTherm, for example floor heating without its own pump: the flow never gets warmer than this, and never warmer than the installation's own maximum. At least 2 °C above where heating starts (Smart heating).

      Outside temperature

      Only used when the installation does not give an outside temperature over OpenTherm. For the maximum flow temperature by the outside temperature, and for switching to cooling automatically.

      Logs

      A line for each change of the target, preset, hold or week program, with where it came from: the screen, the web interface, Home Assistant, an app, or the week program itself. Kept on the Toon, also over a restart. Off by default.

      What MQTT does here
          
      Publishes the ReToon's sensors, heating and thermostat to Home Assistant (auto discovery). Devices that
          announce themselves on this broker (zigbee2mqtt, Tasmota and others) can be put on your pages too: look for
          "(MQTT)" when you add a card.
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        
      Your own MQTT values
      
        
      For topics that do not announce themselves. Each one becomes a value you can put on a page.
      
        
      
        
      
        
      
      
      
      
      
        
      This ReToon's own broker
      
        
      
        
        
      
          
          
        
      
        
        
      What it is for
          
      The ReToon has its own MQTT broker. The programs and apps on the ReToon use it among
          themselves; on its own it cannot be reached from the network. Opened to the network, your devices and
          scripts can use it too, with the user name and password above, on port 1883. The ReToon's values are under
          retoon/ (for example retoon/climate), and retoon/boiler/set takes thermostat commands such as
          "target 20.5". Without a broker filled in above, the screen shows the devices that announce themselves on
          this one.

      Meter adapters
      
      
      
      
      
      How to use this in Home Assistant
        
      In Home Assistant, add the Z-Wave integration (Z-Wave JS) and give it the address shown
        above instead of a USB stick. Devices that are already in this ReToon's Z-Wave network stay in it. The
        connection has no password and is not encrypted: fill in the address of Home Assistant so nothing else
        can use it.
      
      
      
      
      Network keys of the original software
        
      Devices that were added with encryption only talk to Z-Wave JS when it has these keys:
        fill them in under the Z-Wave integration's security keys (S0 legacy, S2 unauthenticated, authenticated,
        access control).
      
        
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Bluetooth proxy
      
      
      
      
      
      
      
      How to use this in Home Assistant
        
      The ReToon presents itself to Home Assistant as an ESPHome Bluetooth proxy. Home Assistant
        finds it by itself: under Settings, Devices & services it appears as a discovered ESPHome device; press
        Add. Bluetooth devices near this ReToon (thermometers, plant sensors, trackers, Shelly BLU, SwitchBot...)
        then work in Home Assistant as if they were next to it. Scanning only runs while Home Assistant is
        connected. The connection has no password and is not encrypted: fill in the address of Home Assistant so
        nothing else can use it.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Zigbee or Thread
      
      
      
      
      The ReToon has one radio for Zigbee and Thread; it does one of the two.
      
      
      
      
      
      
      How to use this in zigbee2mqtt
        
      In zigbee2mqtt, set the adapter to "zoh" and the port to the address shown above.

      Name of this ReToon
      
      
      
      
      About the name
        
      Small letters, digits and "-". The ReToon is then found at https://<name>.local/ and
        makes a new certificate, so the browser warns once more. Your router shows the new name after the next
        restart. Empty puts the standard name back.
      
      
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Starting up
      
      
      
      
      While this is off, the next restart starts the original Toon software again, and it stays
      there: ReToon then has to be started from that system once more. Turn it on once ReToon works the way you
      want.
      
    
      

    
      
      
      Factory defaults
      
      
      Removes every setting of this ReToon: WiFi, Home Assistant, MQTT, your pages, the thermostat
      program, the name and both passwords. The ReToon restarts and shows its WiFi screen, as on its first start.
      The original Toon software is not touched.

      toon-sound play file.wav, toon-sound beep and
        toon-sound tone 440 500 play sounds at this volume. The Toon 1 has a small speaker; the Toon 2
        plays through its buzzer, which sounds thinner and is loudest for high tones.
